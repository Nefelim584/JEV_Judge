# v0.2.0 — Stage B: judge data and calibration

Second jev-lite model: Stage A further trained on public **judge** data (run `stage_b-full-s42-r1`), and
post-hoc calibration shipped with the weights.

**Model:** [Nefelim584/jev-lite](https://huggingface.co/Nefelim584/jev-lite), revision `v0.2.0`. The Hub repo
is renamed from `jev-lite-stage-a` (old links redirect); Stage A stays available as revision `v0.1.0`.
**Method:** [`docs/EVALUATION.md`](../EVALUATION.md) · **Data and licenses:** [`docs/DATASETS.md`](../DATASETS.md)

## Highlights

- **Better judge, mostly where it matters for RAG.** On held-out judge benchmarks, ContextualJudgeBench
  rises from 0.332 to 0.454 consistent accuracy (refusals 0.28 → 0.59), and the ranking of faithful vs
  unfaithful answers improves on FaithBench, HaluBench and held-out RAGBench (AUROC +0.06 to +0.07).
- **Calibrated probabilities by default.** `calibration.json` ships with the weights and
  `Predictor.from_final` applies it: Choice ECE 0.115 → 0.026, Bool 0.058 → 0.014 on the Stage A tests,
  with no answer changed.
- **An optional judge layer** for faithfulness claims fixes most of the "too credulous" threshold on RAG
  answers (FaithBench balanced accuracy +6.3, RAGBench held-out +5.2).
- **The price:** small losses on the Stage A primitives (Bool −1.3, Choice −1.1) and a threshold that
  still drifts per domain (see Known limitations).

## Results

All numbers at 8192 tokens, one seed. Stage A, NLI and Laya are re-scored on the same records from their
saved predictions. Intervals: 95% bootstrap; Δ are paired.

### Judge benchmarks, eval v2 held-out slice (21,054 records, never trained on)

| Benchmark · metric | v0.2.0 | v0.1.0 | NLI | Laya |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CJB · consistent accuracy | **0.454** [0.431, 0.476] | 0.332 | 0.090 | 0.024 |
| FaithBench · balanced accuracy | 0.566 | **0.579** | 0.514 | 0.480 |
| FaithBench · AUROC | **0.681** | 0.619 | 0.481 | 0.506 |
| HaluBench · balanced accuracy | **0.795** | 0.732 | 0.734 | 0.645 |
| HaluBench · AUROC | **0.889** | 0.815 | 0.836 | 0.719 |
| RAGBench, 5 held-out subsets · balanced accuracy | 0.558 | 0.558 | 0.519 | **0.571** |
| RAGBench, 5 held-out subsets · AUROC | **0.643** | 0.583 | 0.578 | 0.581 |

- **CJB per split:** refusal on unanswerable questions 0.280 → 0.588, on answerable 0.460 → 0.608,
  completeness QA 0.317 → 0.533; conciseness is still weak (QA 0.197, summaries 0.291) and faithfulness of
  summaries dips (0.465 → 0.398). At 1024 tokens CJB is 0.479.
- **HaluBench: most of the gain is a seen source.** HaluEval (two thirds of HaluBench) is built on
  HotpotQA, which Stage B trains on: 0.788 → 0.865. Without HaluEval the gain is +2.1 [+0.5, +3.5]; on the
  unseen CovidQA + DROP + FinanceBench it is a tie (−1.1 [−3.1, +1.0]). Long-context CovidQA: 0.794
  (0.617 when cut to 1024).
- **FaithBench and RAGBench held-out:** balanced accuracy is a tie, ranking is clearly better: the model
  says "supported" too often, so the default 0.5 threshold wastes the better ranking (fixed in part by the
  judge layer below).

**In-distribution slice** (RAGBench test of the 7 trained subsets, 7,630 records; not a held-out result):
balanced accuracy 0.586 vs 0.540, AUROC **0.829 vs 0.547**; tables and computed numbers (FinQA, TAT-QA)
go from AUROC 0.49–0.61 to 0.72–0.78.

## What's new

**Model and data**
- Stage B: 2 epochs on 148k records per epoch at 1024 tokens (Bool 105k / Choice 22.5k / Score 20.8k),
  started from the v0.1.0 weights. Eleven judge sources built into records by code (RAGBench train,
  HotpotQA, TAT-QA, FinQA, ClapNQ rebuilt, Faithfulness-QA, MiniCheck D2C, Trust-Data, PsiloQA, Qasper,
  RAGTruth summaries), a replay of the Stage A data (26% of the records) against forgetting, and every
  Score record at most once per run (`run_repeat`; Stage A repeated HelpSteer ~3×).
- Training recipe: Kaggle 2×T4, DDP, fp16, 8-bit AdamW, encoder lr 3e-5, **heads lr 1e-3**, batch 8 per GPU
  (effective 32), 9,268 steps in 7 h 27 min.

**Library**
- `Predictor.from_final(path, layers=...)` loads a model folder with its `calibration.json` (model layer by
  default, `layers=LAYERS` adds the judge layer, `layers=()` gives raw outputs).
- `jevlite.calibration`: fitting and applying both layers on saved predictions; `scripts/calibrate.py`.
- `model.init: checkpoint` with `model.init_checkpoint` (a local folder or `hub:<owner>/<repo>/<folder>`):
  a stage starts from an earlier one.
- Judge-benchmark reports with eval v2 slices and response time (p50 / p95 / mean) per model and slice.

## Fixed

- **The heads barely trained in Stage A.** With one learning rate (3e-5) for the encoder and the small
  heads, the heads stayed close to their initialisation. The trunk and heads now have their own learning
  rate (`train.lr.heads`; 1e-3 in Stage B) and the log reports `head_drift` (Stage B: 1.45, flat after
  epoch 1). With `train.lr.heads: null` training is the same as before.
- **ClapNQ passage shortcut**: answerability rebuilt so that both labels share one passage pool.
- **Duplicate eval records**: Stage B replayed the Stage A tests while HelpSteer2/3 cut their own tests
  from the same files; each eval record id now lives in exactly one eval split.
- **Records that cannot fit**: a judge record whose question alone is longer than `max_len` is dropped at
  mix time (it used to stop training on the GPU); multi-chunk judge sources keep only records that fit
  untruncated (`fit_only`).
- **RAGBench splits** by question text with unique record ids (rows with repeated ids are de-duplicated).
- **Silent failures under DDP**: an error now prints its traceback and exits at once instead of waiting in
  `destroy_process_group`; `--dump-stacks` writes per-rank stacks for hangs.
- **Calibration no longer lives only in reports**: it is shipped with the weights and applied at inference.

## Known limitations

- **The decision threshold drifts per domain.** Without the judge layer the model says "supported" to
  93–96% of RAGBench answers and catches only 18–20% of the unsupported ones; with it, it becomes too strict
  on some sources (HaluBench FinanceBench: "supported" 44% → 6%). One global threshold cannot fit every
  domain; a threshold fitted on a few in-domain labels is the reliable fix.
- **Part of the headline gain is a seen source** (HaluBench HaluEval, via HotpotQA), and RAGBench's 7
  trained subsets are in-distribution. The held-out RAGBench part is small (±0.04).
- **Primitives regressed slightly** (Bool −1.3, Choice −1.1 vs v0.1.0, worst on reasoning multiple-choice).
- **Conciseness is weak** (CJB 0.20–0.29): with both answers visible the model prefers the longer one.
- **Tables and computed numbers** improved only where trained (FinQA / TAT-QA in-distribution); HaluBench
  DROP and FinanceBench, which need arithmetic, stay near chance.
- **Long context:** trained at 1024 tokens; it runs to 8192 and helps on long inputs, but there was no
  long-context training. 6–8k-token inputs take ~1.1 s on a T4; speed on newer GPUs is not measured yet.
- **Labels partly from LLMs**: RAGBench (training and eval), MiniCheck and PsiloQA labels are GPT-4-made;
  agreement with them is agreement with an LLM judge, not with people.
- **One run, one seed; English only; public proxies only.** Quality on a real production judge task has
  not been measured. No learned confidence head (the confidence is the entropy heuristic on calibrated
  probabilities).
- **No share-alike-free variant**: CC BY-SA training data is used, as in v0.1.0.
